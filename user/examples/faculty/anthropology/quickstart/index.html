Anthropology Word-Weaving: Quickstart

Original Assignment Credit: This assignment is adapted from Mike Wesch’s Word-Weaving exercise: https://anth101.com/challenge4/ 

Use this guide if: Your creative assignment has become generic due to AI, and you want to redesign it so AI enhances rather than replaces student thinking.

The Core Idea

Before: Take-home creative assignment → students use AI to generate generic responses → learning disappears

After: In-class creative work (no AI) → take-home reflection with AI as conversation partner → authentic learning returns

Quick Adaptation Steps

1. Identify What Needs to Happen In-Class

Ask: What’s the creative/personal work that only the student can do?

For this assignment:

  • Inventing the phrase/metaphor (requires personal/cultural knowledge)
  • Explaining its meaning and intent (requires self-awareness)

In your discipline: What requires personal insight, lived experience, or unique perspective?

2. Move That Work In-Class

Structure:

  • Devices stowed
  • Clear constraints and examples
  • Time for creation + explanation
  • Submit before leaving

Result: Students create something authentically theirs that AI can’t replicate

3. Reposition AI for Reflection

Instead of: “Use AI to do the work”

Try: “Use AI to reflect on your work”

For this assignment:

  • Test your phrase with family/friends (human reaction)
  • Discuss your phrase with ChatGPT (AI reaction)
  • Compare and reflect on both

Result: AI becomes a tool for thinking, not a replacement for thinking

4. Assess the Reflection

Don’t assess: The phrase itself (could still be AI-generated if you’re not careful)

Do assess:

  • Depth of reflection on testing the phrase
  • Comparison of human vs. AI responses
  • Personal insights about language and meaning
  • Critical thinking about their own creation

Example Prompts for Students

For ChatGPT Conversation (Step 3)

Prompt 1: Understanding

I created a new phrase/metaphor: "[your phrase]"

It means: [brief explanation]

How do you understand this expression? What do you think it communicates?

Prompt 2: Usage & Refinement

Based on your understanding, where or how do you think this phrase could be used?

Do you have suggestions for how it could be improved or made clearer?

Prompt 3: Comparison

When I introduced this to my [family/friends], they [describe reaction].

How does your understanding compare to how humans reacted to it?

Red Flags & Fixes

Red Flag: Students still submit generic AI-generated phrases

Fix: Make in-class portion worth significant points (this example: 4% in-class, 6% take-home) AND collect it before they leave class

Red Flag: Students are lost or uncomfortable with open-ended creativity

Fix: Provide more constraints:

  • Examples from course content
  • Specific categories (love, education, conflict, etc.)
  • Audience guidance (family, friends, peers)
  • Cultural/linguistic examples to inspire

Red Flag: Reflections are still superficial

Fix: Ask for specific comparisons:

  • “Quote what your friend said vs. what ChatGPT said”
  • “What surprised you about each response?”
  • “Which response felt more useful? Why?”

Time Investment

Prep:

  • 30 minutes to redesign assignment structure
  • 10 minutes to create ChatGPT prompt examples

In-Class:

  • 5 minutes: Explain assignment
  • 30-40 minutes: Students create and explain phrase
  • 5 minutes: Collect submissions

Grading:

  • Part 1 (in-class): Quick read for completion + personal engagement
  • Part 2 (take-home): Assess depth of reflection and comparison

Why This Works

  1. Authentic creativity happens first - Students can’t outsource the core creative work
  2. AI becomes useful - Conversation with ChatGPT reveals student thinking
  3. Assessment captures learning - You’re grading reflection, not just the phrase
  4. Students re-engage - Personal investment returns when they create something unique

Adaptation Template

Your creative assignment:

[What do students currently create?]

What could move in-class:

[The core creative/personal work]

How AI could support reflection:

[How students could discuss their work with AI]

What you’d assess:

[The reflection, comparison, or critical thinking]

Next Steps

  1. Review your assignment - where is AI replacing student thinking?
  2. Identify the creative core that needs to happen in-class
  3. Design reflection prompts that use AI as conversation partner
  4. Try it with one section first, gather feedback
  5. Iterate based on what you observe

Need more detail? See the full simulation for complete walkthrough with student examples and assessment rubric.

Key insight: AI doesn’t have to kill creative assignments. It just means we need to protect the creative foundation and use AI for what it’s good at - reflection and conversation.
