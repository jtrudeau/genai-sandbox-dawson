Anthropology Word-Weaving: AI as Reflective Partner

Original Assignment Credit: This assignment is adapted from Mike Wesch’s Word-Weaving exercise. Original assignment: https://anth101.com/challenge4/  The GenAI adaptation described here was developed at Dawson College to address challenges that emerged when AI tools became widely available to students.

Course: Introduction to Anthropology (381-1N1-DW) Context: 40 students, intro-level course Challenge: Take-home assignment rendered ineffective by generic AI-generated responses Solution: In-class creative work + AI as conversation partner for refinement

The Problem

Original assignment: Students invent a new phrase/metaphor, introduce it to family/friends, and reflect on the experience (all take-home).

What broke when AI arrived:

  • Students submitted generic AI-generated phrases
  • Reflections were superficial and AI-written
  • Multiple students turned in identical examples
  • The learning disappeared - no authentic engagement with language creation or cultural observation

The assignment had become: “Ask ChatGPT to invent a phrase and write a reflection about it.”

The Redesign

Key insight: The creative work needs to happen where AI can’t do it for them. Then AI becomes a reflective conversation partner instead of a content generator.

New structure:

  • Part 1 (4%, in-class): Steps 1-2 done in class, devices stowed

    • Invent your phrase/metaphor
    • Write explanation of meaning and intent

  • Part 2 (6%, take-home): Steps 3-4 with AI as assistant

    • Introduce phrase to family/friends
    • Have conversation with ChatGPT about your phrase
    • Reflect on both interactions

Why This Works

Authentic creativity first:

  • Students create their phrase in class without AI
  • The work is theirs - personal, cultural, multilingual
  • Many Dawson students draw on multiple languages, making rich cross-cultural inventions

AI as reflective partner:

  • ChatGPT helps students consider their own perspective
  • Students compare human reactions (family/friends) with AI response
  • Reflections become personal, not generic

Assessment captures learning:

  • Instructor evaluates the reflection and conversation, not just the phrase
  • Looking for: personal engagement, critical thinking about language, cultural awareness

What Changed for Students

Before (all take-home):

  • Generic, AI-generated phrases
  • Superficial reflections
  • No personal investment

After (in-class + AI assist):

  • Students love the assignment
  • Creative, personal phrases (often multilingual)
  • Rich reflections on language, culture, and meaning
  • ChatGPT conversation surfaces their own thinking instead of replacing it

Student experience:

  • Initially reticent to be creative (need constraints and guidance)
  • Become engaged once they understand the structure
  • Many interesting multilingual inventions emerge
  • Personal reflections show genuine learning

Workflow Overview

Step 1 (In-Class): Invent

Create a new phrase or metaphor. Think about linguistic metaphors (arguments, love, education, peace, etc.) and how language shapes thinking.

Step 2 (In-Class): Explain

Write 1-2 paragraphs explaining: What do you want to communicate? Who’s your audience? What inspired this? What reception do you expect?

Step 3 (Take-Home): Test & Converse

  • Introduce phrase to family/friends as if it always existed
  • Ask ChatGPT how it understands your expression
  • Have conversation with ChatGPT about usage and potential improvements

Step 4 (Take-Home): Reflect

Write 2 paragraphs: What happened with family/friends? What did ChatGPT think? Would you improve your phrase? Why or why not?

Key Adaptations

Providing constraints:

  • Clear step-by-step structure
  • In-class time with facilitator available
  • Examples from course readings (metaphors we live by)
  • Guidance on audience and respectfulness

Without constraints: Students felt lost, unsure how to be creative

With constraints: Students engage productively within the structure

Implementation Resources

  • Quickstart Guide - How to adapt this for your course
  • Full Simulation - Complete walkthrough with prompts

Key Takeaways

For faculty considering similar redesigns:

  1. Identify where AI undermines learning - Was it the take-home creativity that became generic?

  2. Move creative work in-class - Make the unique, personal thinking happen without AI access

  3. Reposition AI as conversation partner - Use AI for reflection, comparison, refinement - not generation

  4. Assess the reflection, not just the product - Look for personal engagement and critical thinking

  5. Provide structure - Clear steps and constraints help students engage creatively

Adaptation Questions

Could this work in your discipline?

  • Where do you ask students to create something original?
  • Where has AI made assignments feel generic or superficial?
  • What could students create in-class that’s authentically theirs?
  • How could AI become a reflective partner instead of a generator?

Examples across disciplines:

  • Literature: Create original metaphor in-class → discuss with AI → analyze how AI interprets it
  • Philosophy: Develop ethical dilemma in-class → test with AI → reflect on AI’s reasoning
  • Sociology: Design survey question in-class → ask AI to critique it → refine based on conversation
  • Communication: Draft persuasive message in-class → test AI responses → analyze rhetorical effectiveness

This example demonstrates that AI doesn’t have to undermine creative work - it can enhance reflection when the creative foundation is built first.
