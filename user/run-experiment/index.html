👤 For EducatorsRun an Experiment

Run an Experiment

Use this page to plan, run, and review one small generative AI experiment tied to your own work.

Start with a Goal

Pick something you already do: a task, a draft, a resource, or a situation you want to improve. You might bring an assignment, a reading, a rubric, draft instructions, or notes from a consultation.

Before you open an AI tool, get specific enough about your situation that you know what to ask for and what to look for in the result. The planner below is meant to help structure that process, from preparation through experimentation and reflection.

From vague idea to first use
Too vague to try:
“Use AI to make quiz questions”
Specific enough to try and review:
  • What this is about: 5 short review questions on cell transport that surface common confusions between diffusion, osmosis, and active transport
  • First use: next week’s Intro Biology class, as a pre-class check
  • Who this is for: first-year students who memorize definitions but can’t distinguish the mechanisms
  • What I will look for: whether the questions reveal misconceptions without being too hard, and whether they match what we covered in class

Specific details make the response easier to judge.

If your idea is still vague, narrow it: what is the first specific thing? Who is it for? What would you check?

Not sure where to start? Use Places to Start.

Using the Planner

The planner has five stages:

  1. Choose One Small Experiment: get specific about what you want to try
  2. Prepare the Experiment: gather what you and the AI each need
  3. Run the Experiment: try it and record what happens
  4. Review What Happened: note what worked and what did not
  5. What Might Be Next: decide if and how to continue

Start with stages 1 and 2. You can return to stages 3 to 5 after you have tried something.

From Planning to Running

When stages 1 and 2 feel solid, open your AI tool and run the experiment. What you prepared is the raw material for a good starting prompt. Use Copy This Section to bring your context in directly, or build a prompt from it in your own words.

When you are done, come back to stage 3 to record what happened and what you noticed.

For examples of how to structure the interaction, see Workshop Prompts. For broader guidance, see Working with AI.

Interactive Planner

Your work is saved in this browser while you type. Print or save a PDF draft if you may switch browsers or computers. Your planner is your portable record. If a conversation ends or you switch tools, paste it into a new session and continue. See Working with AI for more.

Before you paste anything into an AI tool: remove student or personal information, use an excerpt or summary if the full material is not appropriate, and check any department or institution guidance that applies.

Getting AI help while you plan
  1. Open the planner section you are working on.
  2. Use Copy This Section.
  3. Add any notes or source material the LLM needs.
  4. Say what kind of help you want.
  5. Review and decide what to keep, change, or reject.

Starting cold? Begin with: I am planning a generative AI experiment for [course, program, support role, or project].
