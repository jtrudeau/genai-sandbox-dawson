# Run an Experiment

Use this page to plan, run, and review one small generative AI experiment tied to your own work.

<div id="from-discovery-to-first-run" />

## Start with a Goal

Pick something you already do: a task, a draft, a resource, or a situation you want to improve. You might bring an assignment, a reading, a rubric, draft instructions, or notes from a consultation.

Before you open an AI tool, get specific enough about your situation that you know what to ask for and what to look for in the result. The planner below helps you get there.

<div className="my-6 p-4 bg-blue-50 dark:bg-blue-950 rounded-lg border-l-4 border-blue-500">
  <strong>From vague idea to first use</strong>
  <div className="mt-3">
    <div className="text-sm font-medium text-gray-500 dark:text-gray-400 mb-1">Too vague to try:</div>
    <div className="italic">&quot;Use AI to make quiz questions&quot;</div>
  </div>
  <div className="mt-4">
    <div className="text-sm font-medium text-gray-500 dark:text-gray-400 mb-1">Specific enough to try and review:</div>
  </div>
  <ul className="mt-1 space-y-1">
    <li><strong>What this is about:</strong> 5 short review questions on cell transport that surface common confusions between diffusion, osmosis, and active transport</li>
    <li><strong>First use:</strong> next week&apos;s Intro Biology class, as a pre-class check</li>
    <li><strong>Who this is for:</strong> first-year students who memorize definitions but can&apos;t distinguish the mechanisms</li>
    <li><strong>What I will look for:</strong> whether the questions reveal misconceptions without being too hard, and whether they match what we covered in class</li>
  </ul>
  <div className="mt-3 text-sm text-gray-600 dark:text-gray-400">
    Specific details improve the quality of the output.
  </div>
</div>

If your idea is still vague, narrow it: what is the first specific thing? Who is it for? What would you check?

Not sure where to start? Use [Places to Start](/user/use-cases).

## Using the Planner

The planner has five stages:

1. Choose One Small Experiment — get specific about what you want to try
2. Prepare the Experiment — gather what you and the AI each need
3. Run the Experiment — try it and record what happens
4. Review What Happened — note what worked and what did not
5. What Might Be Next — decide if and how to continue

Start with stages 1 and 2. You can return to stages 3–5 after you have tried something.

## From Planning to Running

When stages 1 and 2 feel solid, open your AI tool and run the experiment. What you prepared is the raw material for a good starting prompt — use Copy This Section to bring your context in directly, or build a prompt from it in your own words.

When you are done, come back to stage 3 to record what happened and what you noticed.

For examples of how to structure the interaction, see [Workshop Prompts](/workshop-in-a-box/workshop-prompts). For broader guidance, see [Working with AI](/user/working-with-ai).
