23:T27dd,# Session 4: Reflection, Sharing & Sustaining Your Practice


**Welcome to our final session.** You've run experiments, documented what happened, iterated, and learned about how generative AI can be used in teaching and learning.

This session is structured around three questions:
1. What did you learn?
2. What are you taking forward?
3. How will you sustain this practice?

---

## Opening: Collective Reflection

Let's start by acknowledging the work you've done.

**Go around the circle** (or chat if virtual). Each person shares:

**"When I started this workshop, I thought... Now I think..."**

*Examples:*
- "I thought students would cheat more. Now I think transparency and good design matter more than policing."
- "I thought I needed to become an AI expert. Now I think I just need to stay curious and keep experimenting."

*[1-2 minutes per person—no need to elaborate, just name the shift]*

---

Individual reflection time. Use these prompts to capture your learning. This is your documentation for future reference.

### About AI in Teaching

**What did you learn about when/where AI is useful in your teaching?**

```
[Your observations]
```

**What did you learn about the limitations or risks of AI in education?**

```
[What to watch out for]
```

**What surprised you most (in a good way or challenging way)?**

```
[Unexpected insights]
```

---

### About Your Students

**How do your students respond to AI in your class?**

```
[What you observed about student reactions, engagement, learning]
```

**What did you learn about what students need in order to use AI productively?**

```
[Scaffolding, transparency, guidance, constraints?]
```

---

### About Your Teaching

**What did this process reveal about your teaching (beyond just AI)?**

```
[What did experimenting make visible about your pedagogy, values, or practice?]

**What teaching practices do you want to strengthen or reconsider?**

```
[What this work highlighted]

**What did you learn about the process of trying new things in your teaching?**

```
[About risk, iteration, documentation, reflection]
```

**What made experiments easier or harder?**

```
[Time, support, structure, mindset?]
```

*[10-12 minutes of individual writing]*

## Activity 2: Gallery of Experiments

Now let's share what we've learned with each other.

*[Facilitator: Depending on group size, choose one of these formats]*

### Option A: Experiment Showcase (Smaller Groups)

Each person presents their most interesting experiment (3-4 minutes):

1. **What you tried** - Quick context
2. **What happened** - Key observations
3. **One insight you'd share with someone else trying this** - Your main takeaway
4. **Questions you're still sitting with** - What you're still figuring out

*[Facilitator: After each presentation, invite 1-2 clarifying questions from the group]*

---

### Option B: Learning Stations (Larger Groups)

*[Facilitator: Set up 4-5 "stations" in the room (or breakout rooms virtually). Each station has a theme. Participants rotate through 2-3 stations of their choice.]*

**Station themes:**

1. **AI for Assignment Design** - Experiments about creating, refining, or scaffolding assignments
2. **AI for Feedback & Assessment** - Experiments about grading, commenting, or identifying patterns in student work
3. **Student-Facing AI Use** - Experiments where students used AI directly
4. **Unexpected Outcomes** - Things that surprised you, worked differently than expected, or failed productively
5. **Ethical & Equity Considerations** - Experiments that raised questions about fairness, access, or values

At each station:
- Participants share their experiments related to the theme (2 minutes each)
- Group discusses patterns, questions, and insights (5-7 minutes)

---

## Activity 3: What Are You Taking Forward?

Now that you've reflected and heard from others, decide: **What will you continue? What will you let go? What will you try next?**

### Continuation Plan

Answer these questions for yourself:

**1. What practice or experiment are you committed to continuing?**

Be specific. Not "I'll keep using AI". More like "I'll continue using AI to generate discussion prompts, which I'll review and adapt before class."

```
[Your specific commitment]
```

---

**2. What will you stop doing or avoid?**

What didn't work? What's not worth your time? What misaligned with your values?

```
[What you're letting go]
```

---

**3. What's your next experiment?**

If you were going to try one more thing in the next few weeks, what would it be?

```
[Your next small experiment]
```

---

**4. What support do you need to sustain this practice?**

Think about:
- Time (how much is realistic?)
- Community (who can you talk to about this?)
- Resources (what tools, examples, or readings would help?)
- Permission (from yourself, your department, your institution?)

```
[What would make this sustainable]
```

---

**5. How will you know if this is working?**

What will you pay attention in the future? How will you notice if these experiments are improving your teaching and your students' learning—or not?

```
[Your indicators of success]
```

## Building a Community of Practice

You don't have to do this alone. Let's talk about how to stay connected and support each other.

### Options for Ongoing Connection

*[Facilitator: Propose options relevant to your context and gauge interest]*

**Option 1: Monthly Check-ins**
- Informal gatherings (virtual or in-person) to share new experiments and troubleshoot challenges
- Low commitment, drop-in format

**Option 2: Async Discussion Space**
- Shared document, Slack channel, or email list for sharing resources, questions, and updates
- Post when you have something to share or ask

**Option 3: Peer Observation**
- Pair up with someone to observe each other's AI experiments in action
- Provide feedback and share observations

**Option 4: Experiment Library**
- Collaboratively build a collection of documented experiments for others to learn from
- Contribute your templates, reflections, and insights

**Option 5: Reading/Discussion Group**
- Occasional meetings to discuss articles, policies, or emerging issues around AI in education
- Blend theory with your practical experience

*[Facilitator: Collect interest and set up agreed-upon structures]*

## Addressing Lingering Questions

We've covered a lot, but you likely still have questions. Let's surface them.

**Open Q&A** (20-30 minutes)

*[Facilitator: Invite participants to ask questions—about AI, pedagogy, policy, ethics, logistics, or anything else. Treat this as collaborative troubleshooting, not expert answers.]*

Common questions from past cohorts:

- "What should I tell students about using AI on assignments?"
- "How do I balance encouraging experimentation with maintaining academic integrity?"
- "What if my department or institution has restrictive AI policies?"
- "How do I know when I'm using AI ethically vs. just conveniently?"
- "What if students are more skilled with AI than I am?"
- "How do I keep up with how fast this technology is changing?"

**There are no perfect answers.** What we have is each other's experience, collective wisdom, and a commitment to keep asking good questions.

## What Happens After This Workshop?

### You Have Everything You Need

You have:

- ✅ A method for experimenting (small, reversible, documented, reflective)
- ✅ A template for designing and documenting experiments
- ✅ Experience with at least two experiments
- ✅ A community of people asking similar questions
- ✅ Permission to keep trying, failing, and learning

### The Sandbox Doesn't End

This workshop structure is a starting point, not a finish line. The real work is **sustaining a practice of curiosity and experimentation** in your teaching—regardless of what tools or technologies come next.

**Sandbox mindset:**
- Try something small
- Notice what happens
- Reflect honestly
- Adjust and try again

This works for AI. It works for any new pedagogy. It works for teaching itself.

**Welcome to our final session.** You've run experiments, documented what happened, iterated, and learned about how generative AI can be used in teaching and learning.

This session is structured around three questions:
1. What did you learn?
2. What are you taking forward?
3. How will you sustain this practice?

---

## Opening: Collective Reflection

Let's start by acknowledging the work you've done.

**Go around the circle** (or chat if virtual). Each person shares:

**"When I started this workshop, I thought... Now I think..."**

*Examples:*
- "I thought students would cheat more. Now I think transparency and good design matter more than policing."
- "I thought I needed to become an AI expert. Now I think I just need to stay curious and keep experimenting."

*[1-2 minutes per person—no need to elaborate, just name the shift]*

## Activity 2: Gallery of Experiments

Now let's share what we've learned with each other.

*[Facilitator: Depending on group size, choose one of these formats]*

### Option A: Experiment Showcase (Smaller Groups)

Each person presents their most interesting experiment (3-4 minutes):

1. **What you tried** - Quick context
2. **What happened** - Key observations
3. **One insight you'd share with someone else trying this** - Your main takeaway
4. **Questions you're still sitting with** - What you're still figuring out

*[Facilitator: After each presentation, invite 1-2 clarifying questions from the group]*

---

### Option B: Learning Stations (Larger Groups)

*[Facilitator: Set up 4-5 "stations" in the room (or breakout rooms virtually). Each station has a theme. Participants rotate through 2-3 stations of their choice.]*

**Station themes:**

1. **AI for Assignment Design** - Experiments about creating, refining, or scaffolding assignments
2. **AI for Feedback & Assessment** - Experiments about grading, commenting, or identifying patterns in student work
3. **Student-Facing AI Use** - Experiments where students used AI directly
4. **Unexpected Outcomes** - Things that surprised you, worked differently than expected, or failed productively
5. **Ethical & Equity Considerations** - Experiments that raised questions about fairness, access, or values

At each station:
- Participants share their experiments related to the theme (2 minutes each)
- Group discusses patterns, questions, and insights (5-7 minutes)

## Building a Community of Practice

You don't have to do this alone. Let's talk about how to stay connected and support each other.

### Options for Ongoing Connection

*[Facilitator: Propose options relevant to your context and gauge interest]*

**Option 1: Monthly Check-ins**
- Informal gatherings (virtual or in-person) to share new experiments and troubleshoot challenges
- Low commitment, drop-in format

**Option 2: Async Discussion Space**
- Shared document, Slack channel, or email list for sharing resources, questions, and updates
- Post when you have something to share or ask

**Option 3: Peer Observation**
- Pair up with someone to observe each other's AI experiments in action
- Provide feedback and share observations

**Option 4: Experiment Library**
- Collaboratively build a collection of documented experiments for others to learn from
- Contribute your templates, reflections, and insights

**Option 5: Reading/Discussion Group**
- Occasional meetings to discuss articles, policies, or emerging issues around AI in education
- Blend theory with your practical experience

*[Facilitator: Collect interest and set up agreed-upon structures]*

## Addressing Lingering Questions

**Open Q&A** (20-30 minutes)
