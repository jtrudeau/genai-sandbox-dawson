Welcome Facilitators

Your role: Guide faculty through thoughtful AI experimentation in teaching.

This section provides everything you need to run workshops and support faculty exploration.

📚 Facilitator Skills & Philosophy

Core Facilitation Principles

This is not AI training. You’re creating a learning community where faculty:

  • Experiment with small, reversible changes
  • Document what actually happens
  • Share honestly (failures are valuable data)
  • Build judgment about what serves learning

Your job as facilitator:

  1. Create psychological safety - Safe to fail, safe to question
  2. Guide discovery - Not lecture, but facilitate peer learning
  3. Connect to resources - Point to next steps without overwhelming
  4. Model the sandbox mindset - Curiosity, experimentation, reflection

Key Facilitation Skills

Detailed in the 90-minute workshop guide:

  • Creating psychological safety
  • Asking good questions (not leading)
  • Navigating difficult moments (resistance, over-enthusiasm, criticism)
  • Managing time and energy

🛠️ Supporting Materials

For workshop logistics:

  • Communication Templates - Recruitment, reminders, follow-up emails
  • Coordinator Guide - Institutional coordination
  • Pilot Materials - Dawson College pilot insights

🔧 Adapting for Your Context

The materials are designed to be used as-is, but you can adapt:

Time constraints: 60-minute version, 2-hour version, multi-week series

Department-specific: Narrow the focus for disciplinary teams

Virtual/hybrid: Adaptation guidance in the workshop guides

Follow-up: One-time or ongoing cohort options

See the workshop guides for detailed adaptation guidance.

💡 Quick Reference

First time facilitating?

  1. Read 90-Minute Workshop Guide
  2. Review Workshop Prompts
  3. Practice the facilitator demonstration
  4. Use Communication Templates for logistics

Running extended series?

  1. Read Extended Series Guide
  2. Review all 4 session materials
  3. Plan between-session support
  4. Set up cohort communication channel

Your role is to guide faculty toward thoughtful experimentation, not to convince them to adopt or resist AI. Create the conditions for honest exploration.
