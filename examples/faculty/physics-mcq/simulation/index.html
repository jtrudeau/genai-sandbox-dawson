GENAI Sandbox – Faculty Exploration Template (Physics MCQ Simulation)

Last updated: 2025-09-21

Sandbox Overview

“This sandbox concept is your space to explore AI-enhanced teaching before classroom deployment. Share what you learn, keep materials CC-BY where possible, and flag integrity questions during the monthly pulse. The checklist below keeps governance light; use it as a reminder, not a barrier.”

  • Transparency: I can explain any AI-generated material included here.
  • Student data & privacy: Item analytics anonymized; stored in secured course drive.
  • Licensing: AI-generated content checked for originality; final items tagged with provenance.
  • Notes / Exceptions: Pending confirmation from assessment committee on long-term storage policy.

1. Exploration Goal Snapshot

  • Goal (1–2 sentences): Create a repeatable AI + SME workflow that generates accurate physics MCQs with fair distractors so bi-weekly concept checks stay fresh and high-quality.
  • Intended audience: 36 students in PHYS 201 (Mechanics I); reviewed by lead instructor, two TAs, and course committee faculty.
  • Why this matters now: Instructor spends ~6 hours/week drafting questions; item pool has become stale and leaks into student study forums.
  • Special requirements or constraints (accessibility, departmental needs, etc.): Assessment committee requires provenance documentation; LMS demands WCAG-compliant math formatting; follow Bloom’s taxonomy targets set in syllabus.

  • Iteration notes: Initial AI output misapplied work-energy theorem; prompt now enforces “mechanics units” reminder. Distractor rationale helped highlight missing misconception about normal force direction, leading to new distractor.

3. Outputs & Artifacts

  • Artifact 1 (link / location): MCQ development sheet with AI drafts, SME annotations, and performance summary (examples/physics-mcq-sim-sheet.pdf).
  • Artifact 2 (optional): QA checklist template completed for work-energy theorem question.
  • Supporting media or walkthrough: 2-minute Loom highlighting workflow in Google Sheets + Gradescope analytics view.
  • Audience feedback captured (if any): TAs reported question review time dropped from 20 to 8 minutes; faculty reviewers noted distractors felt “fair but challenging,” student pilot optional depending on sandbox scope.

4. Reflection Check-In

  1. What shifted during the sandbox run?
    • Drafting plus review time cut from ~6 hours to 2.5 hours during committee review; item discrimination estimated at 0.42 in limited student pilot.
  2. How did AI help or hinder your approach?
    • Helped by surfacing misconception-based distractors quickly; hindered when algebra steps in rationale were sloppy—required manual correction.
  3. What will you keep, change, or try next?
    • Keep AI-assisted distractor rationale; change prompt to enforce symbolic accuracy; next, auto-generate brief solution explanations paired with practice feedback.

  • Follow-up questions for coordination (policy, feasibility, other constraints): Need official workflow for assessment committee sign-off; confirm retention period for AI prompts in item bank.

6. Next Experiment Hooks

  • Next idea I want to explore: Use AI to propose targeted remedial questions for students who miss specific distractors.
  • Support I could use (community feedback, resources, tooling): Input from math department on symbolic accuracy prompts; guidance on automating QA checklist via scripts.
  • Planned share-out (department meeting, sandbox cohort session, etc.): Present at physics assessment working group and sandbox orientation.

Optional Persona Prompt Sidebar

  • Starter prompts: “Generate a concept-level MCQ on work-energy theorem that tests understanding of energy transfer in damped oscillations; include common misconceptions in distractors.”
  • Optimizer prompts: “For the correct answer, provide a student-friendly rationale and note which misconception each distractor targets.”
