26:T14c1,# Workshop-in-a-Box

**Goal:** Running a standalone workshop for educators to explore generative AI in teaching and learning.

> This is a complete, ready-to-run 90-minute workshop designed to support low-stakes, responsible experimentation with generative AI in teaching.

It is not a training session on tools, nor a mandate to adopt AI.
It is an invitation to try small, bounded experiments, notice what happens, and decide deliberately what, if anything, is worth carrying forward.

Everything needed to run the workshop is included: participant materials, facilitator guidance, prompts, and follow-up resources.


---

## The Workshop

### [**90-Minute Faculty Sandbox Workshop →**](/workshop-in-a-box/workshop-90min)

*Facilitator note: timing for the model workshop can be adapted to your context and time available.*

The core workshop experience is structured in three parts

- **Part 1 (~20 min):** Framing + Demonstration
- **Part 2 (~50 min):** Hands-on discovery and brainstorming
- **Part 3 (~20 min):** Next steps and resources


Participants begin in the role of learners, then shift to their own teaching contexts.

**During the workshop, participants:**
- Experience generative AI as a thinking partner rather than a shortcut
- Examine affordances and limitations through concrete examples
- Generate small, testable ideas relevant to their own courses
- Exchange perspectives with colleagues exploring similar questions

**By the end of the workshop, participants leave with:**

- A clear understanding of the sandbox approach
- Several documented experiment ideas tailored to their context
- Access to shared templates and resources
- An optional pathway for continued sharing and reflection

---

## Workshop Materials

All materials needed to run the workshop are provided and designed to be used as-is or adapted to local context.

**[Workshop Prompts →](/workshop-in-a-box/workshop-prompts)**

A complete set of prompts used during the session, organized into three groups:
- Facilitator demonstration prompts (for live modeling)
- A shared hands-on prompt that all participants try
- A small set of optional prompts for individual or group exploration

Prompts are intentionally transparent and editable. They are meant to be examined, questioned, and modified. 
Not to be treated as fixed scripts.

**[Facilitator Guide →](/facilitator/facilitator-guide-90min)**

A step-by-step guide for running the 90-minute workshop, including:
- Preparation notes and timing guidance
- Section-by-section facilitation suggestions
- Common points of tension or confusion to watch for
- Options for adapting the session to different audiences or time frames

No specialized technical expertise is required. The guide is written to support facilitators who may be exploring generative AI alongside participants.

---

## Adapting This Workshop

### What to Keep (Core Elements)

These preserve the sandbox philosophy:

- **Hands-on experience** - Faculty use AI as learners first
- **Small experiments** - No pressure for transformation
- **Psychological safety** - Safe to fail, safe to share
- **Peer learning** - Participants teach each other
- **Resource connection** - Clear next steps after the workshop

---

### What to Customize (Flexible)

Adapt for your context:

- **Time** - Extend to 2 hours or compress to 60 minutes (see facilitator guide)
- **Focus** - Department-specific (e.g., physics faculty exploring practice problem generation)
- **Prompts** - Narrow the scenarios for specific goals
- **Format** - In-person, virtual, or hybrid
- **Follow-up** - One-time workshop or ongoing cohort

**See [Facilitator Guide](/facilitator/facilitator-guide-90min) for adaptation guidance.**

---

## 🚀 Getting Started

### For Workshop Facilitators

- Read the [90-minute workshop content](/workshop-in-a-box/workshop-90min) 
- Read the [facilitator guide](/facilitator/facilitator-guide-90min)
- Review the [workshop prompts](/workshop-in-a-box/workshop-prompts) and modify as needed. Test beforehand.
- Adapt timeline if needed (60 min? 120 min?)
- Choose facilitator demonstration scenarios
- Decide on follow-up approach (cohort? one-time?)
- Gather any discipline-specific examples
- Collect feedback for future iterations
- Follow up with next steps and additional resources

---

### For Participants

**If you're faculty exploring this on your own:**

You can work through the [workshop content](/workshop-in-a-box/workshop-90min) and [prompts](/workshop-in-a-box/workshop-prompts) independently, but the real value comes from peer learning.

**Consider:**
- Finding 2-3 colleagues to try this together
- Advocating for your institution to run this workshop
- Joining an existing cohort at another institution

57:T14c1,# Workshop-in-a-Box

**Goal:** Running a standalone workshop for educators to explore generative AI in teaching and learning.

> This is a complete, ready-to-run 90-minute workshop designed to support low-stakes, responsible experimentation with generative AI in teaching.

It is not a training session on tools, nor a mandate to adopt AI.
It is an invitation to try small, bounded experiments, notice what happens, and decide deliberately what, if anything, is worth carrying forward.

Everything needed to run the workshop is included: participant materials, facilitator guidance, prompts, and follow-up resources.


---

## The Workshop

### [**90-Minute Faculty Sandbox Workshop →**](/workshop-in-a-box/workshop-90min)

*Facilitator note: timing for the model workshop can be adapted to your context and time available.*

The core workshop experience is structured in three parts

- **Part 1 (~20 min):** Framing + Demonstration
- **Part 2 (~50 min):** Hands-on discovery and brainstorming
- **Part 3 (~20 min):** Next steps and resources


Participants begin in the role of learners, then shift to their own teaching contexts.

**During the workshop, participants:**
- Experience generative AI as a thinking partner rather than a shortcut
- Examine affordances and limitations through concrete examples
- Generate small, testable ideas relevant to their own courses
- Exchange perspectives with colleagues exploring similar questions

**By the end of the workshop, participants leave with:**

- A clear understanding of the sandbox approach
- Several documented experiment ideas tailored to their context
- Access to shared templates and resources
- An optional pathway for continued sharing and reflection

---

## Workshop Materials

All materials needed to run the workshop are provided and designed to be used as-is or adapted to local context.

**[Workshop Prompts →](/workshop-in-a-box/workshop-prompts)**

A complete set of prompts used during the session, organized into three groups:
- Facilitator demonstration prompts (for live modeling)
- A shared hands-on prompt that all participants try
- A small set of optional prompts for individual or group exploration

Prompts are intentionally transparent and editable. They are meant to be examined, questioned, and modified. 
Not to be treated as fixed scripts.

## Adapting This Workshop

### What to Keep (Core Elements)

These preserve the sandbox philosophy:

- **Hands-on experience** - Faculty use AI as learners first
- **Small experiments** - No pressure for transformation
- **Psychological safety** - Safe to fail, safe to share
- **Peer learning** - Participants teach each other
- **Resource connection** - Clear next steps after the workshop

---

### What to Customize (Flexible)

Adapt for your context:

- **Time** - Extend to 2 hours or compress to 60 minutes (see facilitator guide)
- **Focus** - Department-specific (e.g., physics faculty exploring practice problem generation)
- **Prompts** - Narrow the scenarios for specific goals
- **Format** - In-person, virtual, or hybrid
- **Follow-up** - One-time workshop or ongoing cohort

**See [Facilitator Guide](/facilitator/facilitator-guide-90min) for adaptation guidance.**
