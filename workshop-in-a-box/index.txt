23:T1d0e,# Workshop-in-a-Box: Faculty AI Sandbox

**Your Intent:** Running a standalone workshop for faculty to explore AI in teaching

> **Complete, ready-to-run 90-minute workshop with all materials included**

This is a turnkey workshop designed for faculty to experience the sandbox approach: small, reversible experiments with AI in teaching. Everything you need is here—participant content, facilitator guidance, prompts, and resources.

---

## 🎯 The Workshop

### [**90-Minute Faculty Sandbox Workshop →**](/workshop-in-a-box/workshop-90min)

**The core workshop experience.** Participant-facing content structured as:

**Part 1 (~20 min):** Framing + Demonstration
**Part 2 (~50 min):** Hands-on discovery & brainstorming
**Part 3 (~20 min):** Next steps & resources

**What participants do:**
- Experience using AI thoughtfully (as learners first)
- Brainstorm experiments for their own teaching
- Connect with colleagues exploring similar questions
- Leave with ideas and action steps

**What participants get:**
- Understanding of the sandbox approach
- 5+ experiment ideas generated with AI
- Access to templates and resources
- Optional: cohort for continued sharing (end-of-semester check-in)

## 🎨 Workshop Philosophy

**This is not AI training.** It's a learning community where faculty:
- Experiment with small, reversible changes
- Document what actually happens (not what they hoped)
- Share honestly (failures are valuable data)
- Build judgment about what serves learning

**Grounded in:**
- **Humanism** - Students are humans, not efficiency problems
- **Responsibility** - Acknowledge risks, act with care
- **Transparency** - Be honest with students about what we're trying

---

## 🔧 Adapting This Workshop

### What to Keep (Core Elements)

These preserve the sandbox philosophy:

✅ **Hands-on experience** - Faculty use AI as learners first
✅ **Small experiments** - No pressure for transformation
✅ **Psychological safety** - Safe to fail, safe to share
✅ **Peer learning** - Participants teach each other
✅ **Resource connection** - Clear next steps after the workshop

---

### What to Customize (Flexible)

Adapt for your context:

◯ **Time** - Extend to 2 hours or compress to 60 minutes (see facilitator guide)
◯ **Focus** - Department-specific (e.g., math faculty exploring problem generation)
◯ **Prompts** - Narrow the scenarios for specific goals
◯ **Format** - In-person, virtual, or hybrid
◯ **Follow-up** - One-time workshop or ongoing cohort

**See [Facilitator Guide](/facilitator/facilitator-guide-90min) for detailed adaptation guidance.**

---

## 🚀 Getting Started

### For Workshop Coordinators

**Step 1: Review**
- Read the [90-minute workshop content](/workshop-in-a-box/workshop-90min)
- Review the [workshop prompts](/workshop-in-a-box/workshop-prompts)
- Read the [facilitator guide](/facilitator/facilitator-guide-90min)

**Step 2: Customize**
- Adapt timeline if needed (60 min? 120 min?)
- Choose facilitator demonstration scenario
- Decide on follow-up approach (cohort? one-time?)
- Customize [communication templates](/internal/communication-templates)

**Step 3: Prepare**
- Book room or set up Zoom
- Test the ChatGPT prompts beforehand
- Gather any discipline-specific examples
- Send recruitment emails

**Step 4: Run It**
- Follow the workshop structure
- Encourage honesty and curiosity
- Capture questions and insights
- Adjust in real-time as needed

**Step 5: Follow Up**
- Send next steps email with resources
- Offer optional cohort check-in (end-of-semester)
- Collect feedback for future iterations

---

### For Participants

**If you're faculty exploring this on your own:**

You can work through the [workshop content](/workshop-in-a-box/workshop-90min) and [prompts](/workshop-in-a-box/workshop-prompts) independently, but the real value comes from peer learning.

**Consider:**
- Finding 2-3 colleagues to try this together
- Advocating for your institution to run this workshop
- Joining an existing cohort at another institution

---

## 📊 Success Indicators

**The workshop is working if:**

✅ Participants stay engaged and curious
✅ People generate ideas they actually want to try
✅ Discussion is honest (including concerns and skepticism)
✅ Participants connect with each other, not just the facilitator
✅ Some people continue experimenting after the workshop

**Red flags:**

🚨 Participants are passive or performative
🚨 No one wants to try anything afterward
🚨 Discussion is superficial or overly enthusiastic without critical thought
🚨 Faculty feel pressured to adopt AI, not empowered to experiment

---

## 💡 Why Workshop-in-a-Box?

**Turnkey:** Run as-is with minimal preparation

**Experience-first:** Faculty use AI as learners before thinking about teaching applications

**Practice-based:** Participants leave with ideas they generated, not generic advice

**Sustainable:** Builds a method for ongoing experimentation, not one-time compliance

**Flexible:** Works across disciplines, institutions, and contexts

**Community-oriented:** Supports continued connection beyond the workshop

76:T1d0e,# Workshop-in-a-Box: Faculty AI Sandbox

**Your Intent:** Running a standalone workshop for faculty to explore AI in teaching

> **Complete, ready-to-run 90-minute workshop with all materials included**

This is a turnkey workshop designed for faculty to experience the sandbox approach: small, reversible experiments with AI in teaching. Everything you need is here—participant content, facilitator guidance, prompts, and resources.

---

## 🎯 The Workshop

### [**90-Minute Faculty Sandbox Workshop →**](/workshop-in-a-box/workshop-90min)

**The core workshop experience.** Participant-facing content structured as:

**Part 1 (~20 min):** Framing + Demonstration
**Part 2 (~50 min):** Hands-on discovery & brainstorming
**Part 3 (~20 min):** Next steps & resources

**What participants do:**
- Experience using AI thoughtfully (as learners first)
- Brainstorm experiments for their own teaching
- Connect with colleagues exploring similar questions
- Leave with ideas and action steps

**What participants get:**
- Understanding of the sandbox approach
- 5+ experiment ideas generated with AI
- Access to templates and resources
- Optional: cohort for continued sharing (end-of-semester check-in)

## 🎨 Workshop Philosophy

**This is not AI training.** It's a learning community where faculty:
- Experiment with small, reversible changes
- Document what actually happens (not what they hoped)
- Share honestly (failures are valuable data)
- Build judgment about what serves learning

**Grounded in:**
- **Humanism** - Students are humans, not efficiency problems
- **Responsibility** - Acknowledge risks, act with care
- **Transparency** - Be honest with students about what we're trying

---

## 🔧 Adapting This Workshop

### What to Keep (Core Elements)

These preserve the sandbox philosophy:

✅ **Hands-on experience** - Faculty use AI as learners first
✅ **Small experiments** - No pressure for transformation
✅ **Psychological safety** - Safe to fail, safe to share
✅ **Peer learning** - Participants teach each other
✅ **Resource connection** - Clear next steps after the workshop

---

### What to Customize (Flexible)

Adapt for your context:

◯ **Time** - Extend to 2 hours or compress to 60 minutes (see facilitator guide)
◯ **Focus** - Department-specific (e.g., math faculty exploring problem generation)
◯ **Prompts** - Narrow the scenarios for specific goals
◯ **Format** - In-person, virtual, or hybrid
◯ **Follow-up** - One-time workshop or ongoing cohort

**See [Facilitator Guide](/facilitator/facilitator-guide-90min) for detailed adaptation guidance.**

## 🚀 Getting Started

### For Workshop Coordinators

**Step 1: Review**
- Read the [90-minute workshop content](/workshop-in-a-box/workshop-90min)
- Review the [workshop prompts](/workshop-in-a-box/workshop-prompts)
- Read the [facilitator guide](/facilitator/facilitator-guide-90min)

**Step 2: Customize**
- Adapt timeline if needed (60 min? 120 min?)
- Choose facilitator demonstration scenario
- Decide on follow-up approach (cohort? one-time?)
- Customize [communication templates](/internal/communication-templates)

**Step 3: Prepare**
- Book room or set up Zoom
- Test the ChatGPT prompts beforehand
- Gather any discipline-specific examples
- Send recruitment emails

**Step 4: Run It**
- Follow the workshop structure
- Encourage honesty and curiosity
- Capture questions and insights
- Adjust in real-time as needed

**Step 5: Follow Up**
- Send next steps email with resources
- Offer optional cohort check-in (end-of-semester)
- Collect feedback for future iterations

### For Participants

**If you're faculty exploring this on your own:**

You can work through the [workshop content](/workshop-in-a-box/workshop-90min) and [prompts](/workshop-in-a-box/workshop-prompts) independently, but the real value comes from peer learning.

**Consider:**
- Finding 2-3 colleagues to try this together
- Advocating for your institution to run this workshop
- Joining an existing cohort at another institution

## 📊 Success Indicators

**The workshop is working if:**

✅ Participants stay engaged and curious
✅ People generate ideas they actually want to try
✅ Discussion is honest (including concerns and skepticism)
✅ Participants connect with each other, not just the facilitator
✅ Some people continue experimenting after the workshop

**Red flags:**

🚨 Participants are passive or performative
🚨 No one wants to try anything afterward
🚨 Discussion is superficial or overly enthusiastic without critical thought
🚨 Faculty feel pressured to adopt AI, not empowered to experiment

---

## 💡 Why Workshop-in-a-Box?

**Turnkey:** Run as-is with minimal preparation

**Experience-first:** Faculty use AI as learners before thinking about teaching applications

**Practice-based:** Participants leave with ideas they generated, not generic advice

**Sustainable:** Builds a method for ongoing experimentation, not one-time compliance

**Flexible:** Works across disciplines, institutions, and contexts

**Community-oriented:** Supports continued connection beyond the workshop
