📦 Workshop-in-a-BoxSession Overview

SALTISE 2026 (S-06): The GenAI Sandbox

From Low-Stakes AI Experiments to Facilitating for Your Own Context. Presented at the SALTISE 2026 conference .

Many educators are curious about generative AI but unsure where to start, or whether it belongs in their practice at all. The GenAI Sandbox, developed and piloted at Dawson College, is a practical entry point: structured, low-stakes experimentation that helps educators move from speculation to evidence on their own terms.

This interactive session invites participants to experience the sandbox framework firsthand as designers. Small groups explore a prepared sandbox together, then each participant designs an AI experiment for their own context and receives peer feedback. It is one adaptation of the Workshop-in-a-Box model: it both presents the sandbox and models its use, so participants experience the same group-to-individual structure they could later run with colleagues.

What You Leave With

  • a drafted AI experiment for your teaching, support, or program context
  • a facilitation guide for running a sandbox session with colleagues
  • first-hand experience of the group-to-individual model

Purpose

The sandbox gives educators a structured way to test one use of generative AI before making a larger decision about practice.

In this session, participants work from their own teaching and learning context. The goal is to leave with a draft experiment that is specific enough to review, small enough to try, and clear enough to explain to a colleague.

What Participants Need

  • a laptop and an AI tool they can use, either a facilitator-provided ChatGPT or their own LLM (pairs can share one laptop)
  • a course, assignment, learning activity, support workflow, or program task they know well
  • one possible place where AI might help with preparation, feedback, student sense-making, discussion, or expectations
  • any source material they can safely use, such as assignment instructions, rubric language, activity notes, or a short de-identified excerpt
  • a willingness to review the result against their own professional judgment

Do not paste student or personal information into an AI tool. Use an excerpt, summary, or invented example when needed.

Session Flow

The 75-minute timing below is a suggestion to illustrate the flow. Adapt it to your audience and room constraints.

TimeActivityParticipant work
0-20 minFrame the sandbox and see the examplesChoose one teaching, support, or program task worth testing
20-32 minExplore the sandboxExplore the prepared sandbox together as a table and discuss what the context makes possible
32-48 minDraft one experimentBuild a small experiment for your own context
48-60 minPeer feedbackCheck scope, review criteria, boundaries, and evidence
60-68 minFacilitation handoffIdentify one facilitation technique you could reuse
68-75 minQuestions and bufferAsk questions and save your draft

Materials

  • Sandbox Experiment for the hands-on exercise, drafting, and peer feedback
  • Facilitation Guide for participants who want to run the same structure with colleagues
  • Run an Experiment for continuing after the session

What Would Be a Good Draft?

A good draft is clear enough that another educator could understand what you will try and how you will review it. Rough notes are fine.

By the end of the session, aim to have:

  • one task or artifact
  • one first use
  • the source material or context the AI needs
  • what stays educator judgment
  • one review criterion
  • one reason to continue, revise, or stop

How This Connects To Facilitation

The structure participants experience is the structure they can run with colleagues:

  1. Start from a real task.
  2. Explore a prepared sandbox together.
  3. Move from group discovery to individual design.
  4. Use peer feedback before anyone treats the plan as ready.
  5. Close with the next instructional or support decision.

The session is complete when participants can explain what they are testing, what evidence would matter, and what remains a human decision.
