23:T1145,# Workshop-in-a-Box: 90-Minute Faculty Sandbox

This session is a practical entry point for faculty who want to explore generative AI in teaching and learning without committing to broad change.

The focus is on bounded experiments: try one idea, observe what happens, document evidence, and decide what is worth continuing.

## Session Outcomes

By the end of the session, participants should have:

1. a clear understanding of the sandbox approach
2. firsthand experience using AI in a bounded activity
3. one or more experiment ideas tied to their own context
4. a practical next-step pathway for implementation

## Agenda at a Glance

- **Part 1 (20 min):** Frame the sandbox and run one live demonstration
- **Part 2 (50 min):** Participant exploration, small-group exchange, deeper scenario work
- **Part 3 (20 min):** Synthesis, next steps, and continuity resources

---

## Part 1: Framing and Demonstration (20 minutes)

### Frame the Session

Open with the purpose of the workshop:

- this is not tool training
- this is not a mandate to adopt AI
- this is structured exploration in a low-stakes setting

Introduce the working principles:

- small and reversible experiments
- transparent documentation
- faculty judgment as the decision point
- student learning as the primary criterion

### Demonstrate One Bounded Example

Use one facilitator prompt from [Workshop Prompts](/workshop-in-a-box/workshop-prompts).

As you demo, make your evaluation criteria explicit:

- What is the task?
- What is the quality bar?
- What does the output do well?
- What requires revision or rejection?

Keep the demonstration short. The goal is to model a process, not to optimize a perfect result.

---

## Part 2: Participant Exploration and Sharing (50 minutes)

### Activity A: Shared Hands-On Prompt (10-15 minutes)

Participants complete the Set 2 prompt from [Workshop Prompts](/workshop-in-a-box/workshop-prompts).

Ask participants to fill in their real context, run the prompt, and choose one experiment idea they could plausibly test.

Suggested capture prompts:

- Which idea is most relevant to your context?
- What would need adjustment before trying it?
- What risk or constraint stands out immediately?

### Pair/Small-Group Exchange (5-7 minutes)

In groups of 2-3, each participant shares:

- the idea they selected
- why they selected it
- one concern or open question

### Activity B: Scenario Deep Dive (15-20 minutes)

Participants select one Set 3 scenario prompt (materials, feedback, sense-making, interaction, or integrity) and run a deeper exploration.

Ask participants to identify:

- what is immediately usable
- what needs redesign
- what evidence they would collect in a real run

### Scenario-Based Synthesis (10-12 minutes)

Group participants by scenario.
Each group identifies:

- one promising pattern
- one recurring challenge
- one unresolved question

Then run a brief whole-group share-out.

---

## Part 3: Next Steps and Continuity (20 minutes)

### Consolidate the Session

Summarize what participants have done:

- generated context-specific experiment ideas
- tested prompt workflows against real teaching concerns
- compared interpretations across disciplines

### Move to Action

Invite each participant to commit to one bounded run in the next 2-4 weeks.

Continuation pathway:

- [Run an Experiment](/user/run-experiment)
- [Welcome](/user)
- [Interactive Planner](/user/run-experiment#interactive-planner)

### Final Reflection

Ask participants to record:

1. one insight from the workshop
2. one experiment they intend to try
3. one question they still need to resolve

If useful, collect these reflections to inform follow-up support.

---

## Facilitator Preparation Checklist

Before the session:

- review [Workshop Prompts](/workshop-in-a-box/workshop-prompts)
- select one demo scenario and test it in advance
- decide whether this run is cross-disciplinary or department-specific
- prepare a mechanism for participants to save outputs and notes
- confirm continuity links and follow-up plan
